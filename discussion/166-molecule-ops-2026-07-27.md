## Scope

This document tracks operations over `Molecule`: conversion between implicit
and explicit hydrogen representations, constraint relocation and explicit
discharge, and validation of the concrete domains of inherent entity fields.
It carries forward the still-relevant parts of Phase 11a and Phase 12 from
doc 086 using the current model.

It also tracks enforcement of Element::valence_capacity in the counts resolver
and the operation naming changes below.

The operation boundaries are:

- resolution fills undetermined state using a chemistry model;
- transformation rewrites one resolved representation into another;
- validation checks integrity, model-independent invariants, and
  model-dependent conformance without mutation.

The boundaries and validator composition established by docs 096 and 118 remain
in force. Correspondence-returning hydrogen variants belong to the separate
mutation-witness work. New or revised molecule-level constraint validation,
including unpaired-electron coupling constraints, is deferred to doc 195.

`ConnectivityModel` is already part of `ChemistryModel`, and
`ConnectivityConformanceValidator` already runs first in conformance
validation. Constraint invariants, aromaticity conformance, spin invariants,
overlay uniqueness, and aromatic/multicenter electron-vector shape are also
implemented. This document does not retain parallel work items for them.

## Implicit and explicit hydrogen transformations

Add two high-level transformer types under `umol-graph/src/ops/transform`, each
implementing `Transformer`. They are the ordinary public operation surface and
need no configuration:

- `HydrogenFolder` folds eligible explicit hydrogen atoms and their bonds into
  implicit-hydrogen counts;
- `HydrogenUnfolder` unfolds literal implicit-hydrogen counts into explicit
  hydrogen atoms and bonds.

Implement both operations in umol-graph through the existing Transformer
interface and graph-IR editing operations, following the other transformers.
Their operation errors are FoldHydrogensError and UnfoldHydrogensError.
The common Transform extension trait described below supplies Molecule receiver
methods from umol-graph. There are no standalone folding/unfolding functions
or inherent Molecule methods in graph IR.

### API contract

- The operation objects are stateless engines, not carriers of validated state.
  Their input and output are ordinary closed `Molecule` values.
- Both operations follow the existing complete-plan/application pattern.
  `transform` consumes its molecule and drops it on rejection;
  `transform_into` plans before mutation and preserves its input on rejection,
  without a transaction or rollback journal.
- The first folding or unfolding operation that depends on a nonnegative
  literal implicit-hydrogen count or a consistent stereo ligand census checks
  that precondition itself. A prior validator call is not required.
- The validators inspect model-independent semantic predicates. They neither
  construct a stronger molecule type nor transform, repair, or resolve their
  input. A relevant non-literal field produces `Solution::Underdetermined`; a
  literal violation produces `Solution::Contradictory`.
- `transform_iter` borrows its source and lazily yields one independent
  transformed molecule on success and no item on error. Callers that require
  the typed diagnostic use `transform` or `transform_into`.
- Both transformations preserve `Molecule` representation integrity and are
  idempotent. No inverse, lossless, or general roundtrip law is exposed.
- This work adds no conversions, prepared or validated wrappers, or
  correspondence-returning variants. The common Rust/Python receiver interface
  is settled below and remains outside the hydrogen implementation stages.

### Entity-level constraints

When folding `k` hydrogens onto a retained atom, its implicit-hydrogen count
increases by `k`, while its localized valence and degree decrease by `k`.
Unfolding reverses those changes. Adjust the corresponding entity-level
assertions with the existing numeric-form operations. Total valence, total
degree, total hydrogens, and ring quantities are unchanged. NumForm arithmetic
and symbolic-form handling are unchanged by this work.

Replacing actual and virtual stereo ligands preserves their stored positions
and the stereo configuration. Transport entity-level tetrahedral and cis/trans
assertions when their implicit atom-numbering frame changes.

Molecule-level constraints receive the existing reference transport only.
Their predicates are not rewritten or evaluated by these operations. The
folding exclusion for a hydrogen or bond explicitly referenced by a molecule
constraint remains in force.

### Expansion to explicit hydrogens

- A literal implicit-hydrogen count must be nonnegative; return
  `UnfoldHydrogensError` atomically for a negative count. For `n > 0`, add `n`
  hydrogen atoms and `n` localized single bonds; zero is unchanged.
- Each added hydrogen has the concrete form
  `H#i=#c0#h0#n0#u0#s`.
- Set the source atom's implicit-hydrogen field to literal zero.
- Leave undetermined or non-literal counts unchanged.
- Leave already explicit hydrogens unchanged; mixed explicit and implicit
  hydrogen representations therefore become fully explicit wherever the
  implicit count is literal.
- A stereo-atom frame and each stereo-bond endpoint contain a complete ligand
  census, not a selected subset. For every affected frame or endpoint, its
  number of virtual implicit-hydrogen ligands must equal the bearer's literal
  implicit-hydrogen count. A stereo entity therefore cannot exist on a bearer
  with more than one implicit hydrogen: the complete frame would require
  repeated implicit-hydrogen ligands, which representation integrity
  prohibits.
- Create explicit hydrogens once per bearer atom. Replace every virtual
  implicit-hydrogen occurrence anchored to that bearer with the corresponding
  newly explicit hydrogen. An atom with a literal count greater than one can
  still be unfolded when it is not the bearer of a stereo entity.
- Return `UnfoldHydrogensError` when an affected stereo frame or endpoint
  disagrees with a literal implicit-hydrogen count, or when a virtual ligand is
  backed by a non-literal count. An atom with a non-literal count and no such
  virtual ligand remains unchanged.
- Reapplying the transformer is a no-op.

### Collapse to implicit hydrogens

Collapse a hydrogen only when it is an unambiguous ordinary explicit hydrogen:

- exactly one incident localized bond;
- the bond has the concrete form `1#c0#u0#s` and no bond constraints;
- non-hydrogen partner;
- no isotope label, charge, spin, or other non-default atom state;
- no atom constraints;
- neither the hydrogen nor its bond is referenced by a molecule constraint;
- no participation in dative, aromatic, multicenter, or noncovalent relations.

The partner's implicit-hydrogen count must be a nonnegative literal. An
undetermined count does not authorize deleting an explicit hydrogen; leave
that hydrogen unchanged. A negative literal produces `FoldHydrogensError`
atomically. Otherwise remove the hydrogen and bond and increment the partner's
implicit-hydrogen count. No separate checked-arithmetic or overflow error is
part of this operation's contract.

An explicit hydrogen used as a stereo-atom or stereo-bond ligand is replaced
in place by an implicit-hydrogen ligand on its non-hydrogen partner. If the
complete affected stereo frames cannot represent the result without repeating
an implicit-hydrogen ligand, return `FoldHydrogensError` atomically. This
includes a pre-existing implicit hydrogen and multiple explicit hydrogen
ligands that would be folded onto the same stereo bearer. The transformer does
not remove the stereo entity to make the fold possible.

Preserve D/T, hydrides/protons, radicals, H2, other relation participants, and
any hydrogen whose information could not be represented after collapse. Mixed
explicit and literal implicit hydrogens are combined in the implicit count
when no stereo entity prevents it. Reapplying the transformer is a no-op.

No general losslessness or inverse roundtrip law is claimed. Folding removes
atom and bond identities, and mixed or non-foldable explicit representations
preclude a universal inverse law. Each operation returns only the transformed
`Molecule`; the correspondence or compaction that witnesses the change is
separate mutation-witness work.

Verification must cover per-direction behavior, idempotence, field preservation,
relation preservation, representative reconstructible cases, and every error
boundary. Do not state a general roundtrip property.

## Explicit constraint discharge

Add an explicitly invoked Transformer under `umol-graph/src/ops/transform` that
removes constraints entailed by independently derived molecular information.
This retains the useful redundancy removal of the automatic closing resolver
pass removed in [236](236-operation-ownership-copy-costs-2026-10-09.md), while
keeping discharge an operation the caller requests.

Start in umol-graph, reusing the model-independent constraint-evaluation
machinery. Move a reusable implementation into graph IR only if a later need
justifies it; no graph-IR operation is part of the initial design.

The intended scope includes entity-local assertions and molecule-level logical
constraints. Remove an assertion only when independently derived information
entails it: compatibility is insufficient, and evidence incorporating the
assertion cannot establish its redundancy. Preserve undecidable assertions and
all topology, entity identities, inherent fields, and relation data.

Discharge can act on a partially specified molecule and requires no chemistry
model or prior resolution. The broader definition of Transformer is outside
this work item.

### Agreed discharge contract — 2026-10-18

The parameterless engine is ConstraintDischarger, separate from ConstraintLifter
and ConstraintInliner. It implements Transformer through transform,
transform_into, and transform_iter. Transform exposes discharge_constraints and
discharge_constraints_into on Molecule; both take no operation arguments.
Python exposes the same named methods directly on Molecule, with the ownership
contract of the other transformations. Its contract is:

- Use the open derived reading. An existing overlay can establish a positive
  assertion, but absence alone does not justify deleting a negative assertion.
  Intrinsic fields and actual topology remain usable evidence.
- Exclude all stored assertions from the evidence, both inline and at molecule
  level. This prevents self-justification, mutual justification, and dependence
  on the order in which assertions are considered.
- Require entailment rather than compatibility. A derived set {2, 3} proves
  an assertion allowing {1, 2, 3}, but not one allowing {3, 4}. Use the existing
  lattice matching contract; derived information need not be literal.
- Remove a complete stored entry only when that evidence proves it true.
  Evaluate logical expressions to decide whether the whole entry is redundant;
  retain the original expression when it is not proved. Do not rewrite its
  internal tree as part of this operation.
- Retain false, undecidable, and unsupported assertions. A false assertion is
  available for an explicit validator to report; discharge does not repair it.
  Inconsistent evidence proves nothing, including under negation.
- Apply the established vacuity rule to kindless inline stereo payloads:
  discard them without inspecting their contents. Meaningful stereo assertions
  remain unless independent information proves them; do not use another stored
  assertion as that proof.

ConstraintDischarger is infallible: Transformer::Error is std::convert::Infallible.
An inability to establish redundancy retains the assertion. The Transform
methods therefore return Result<Self, Infallible> and Result<(), Infallible>;
Python returns the transformed molecule or None for the _into method, with no
operation-specific exception.

Use the fixed ring selection prescribed by the constraint contract. Connected
components use the existing Bfs algorithm internally. There is no discharge
configuration or caller-selected algorithm. Ring and component results are
computed lazily and reused within the invocation.

The current ConstraintInvariantsValidator cannot be reused wholesale: its
stereo comparison reads stored assertions, and relational atom predicates use
the closed derived reading. Reuse individual independent readings and evaluation
code where their contracts agree. The discharge evaluator must preserve the
chosen open reading through nested predicates and reuse ring/component results
within one invocation rather than perceiving them separately for each entry.
This does not require a new general-purpose reasoning framework or a change to
the validator's existing behavior.

The current derived readings for constraints on stereo entities are
undetermined. Non-vacuous assertions in those families therefore remain unless
independent information proves them; this operation does not introduce a new
stereo perception or symmetry calculation.

The required laws are preservation of meaning, topology, entity ids, inherent
fields, and relation data; idempotence; and independence from constraint
traversal order. Exact cases must distinguish proved redundancy from mere
compatibility, false predicates, missing information, contradictory readings,
logical negation, and duplicate assertions. S16 implements and verifies these
contracts.

## Stereo ligand mutation

The participant-mutation prerequisite is complete as of 2026-09-17 (S0–S4 below).
S5 adds the approved whole-relation addition/removal surface to all five sets.
The graph-IR/editor mutation surface and its delegation to storage were completed
in 213. S9 uses that surface for the hydrogen transformations.

Both hydrogen transformations require a position-preserving substitution in a
stereo frame: unfolding replaces a virtual implicit-hydrogen ligand with the
new explicit hydrogen atom, while folding makes the inverse representation
change. The ligand occupying the position changes, but the position itself and
the configuration read against the frame do not.

This is distinct from both existing frame operations. `FrameTransport` applies
a supplied frame action, while `Reframe` selects the canonical representative
frame. Both preserve the ligand multiset, transport the frame-relative form,
and reorder the stored ligands. At the start of this work, that storage operation
was named `FixedVarBirelationSet::permute_2_with`; S3a applies the approved rename.
Ligand substitution preserves the frame position but changes the participant
multiset. It may therefore change the derived incidence index: replacing a
virtual ligand anchored to one atom with an actual ligand naming a newly added
hydrogen changes which atom ids the stereo entity references.

The immutable graph-IR readers remain
`StereoAtoms::ligands(id) -> &[StereoLigand]` and
`StereoBonds::ligands(id) -> &[StereoLigand]`. Their storage is backed by the
second factor of `FixedVarBirelationSet`. Before S2, its participants were immutable
except for multiset-preserving permutation. `MoleculeEditor` still
materializes a touched stereo family into owned entries and rebuilds indexed
relation storage when the molecule is published.

## Relation storage mutation

### Storage-owned mutation decision — 2026-09-15

Participant mutation belongs to relation storage. The editor must delegate to
that capability rather than use materialized entries as a substitute for it.
The prerequisite covers all five relation-set shapes: FixedRelationSet,
VarRelationSet, FixedFixedBirelationSet, FixedVarBirelationSet, and
VarVarBirelationSet. The earlier proposal to expose mutable ligand references
from the editor is withdrawn; a bare mutable reference cannot give indexed
storage control over completion of the replacement.

Design whole-factor replacement, single-participant replacement, insertion,
and removal together. Whole-factor replacement is the foundation: fixed
factors retain their arity, variable factors accept a new sequence, and
birelations can replace either factor or both together. Implement the complete
method family initially through whole-factor replacement; specialize later
only if justified.

Replacement preserves the relation id, payload, and supplied participant order.
It updates incidence over the complete relation, including both factors of a
birelation. Incidence is a union of references: replacing one participant must
retain an old node or edge incidence if another participant still references
it. This matters when a virtual stereo ligand and its site share an atom
reference. Graph core neither interprets the payload nor enforces molecular
semantics. Graph IR owns the coordinated topology, ligand, and payload changes
and the molecule publication boundary.

A single helper that scans and rewrites both stereo-atom and stereo-bond
frames is not the intended graph-IR abstraction. The entity families remain
explicit. Hydrogen substitution preserves positions and their configuration;
arbitrary participant replacement makes no such semantic promise. Folding must
substitute hydrogen ligands before topology removal, whose existing cascade
would otherwise remove stereo entities still referencing those hydrogens.

### Approved graph-core additions — 2026-09-15

The following methods are approved public additions in graph core. Every
method takes &mut self followed by id: RelationId; the tables list the
remaining arguments. All return (). An invalid relation id panics, consistently
with existing indexed access. An invalid factor-local position also panics:
replacement/removal require position < length; insertion permits position <=
length.

Participant values follow the same admission contract as new. NodeId and
EdgeId references inside participants are stored and indexed through
RelationParticipant::refs without checking membership in an external graph.
The relation set has no supplied graph or declared node/edge domain against
which to validate them. Repeated participant values remain representable;
incidence deduplicates references, not the stored participant sequence. Fixed
factor lengths are enforced by array types; variable factors permit any length,
including zero. There is no new Result, error type, or checked variant.

#### Single-participant operations

All of these operations are included in the initial implementation. They
construct the replacement factor and delegate to whole-factor replacement.

| Available on | Methods | Arguments after id |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FixedRelationSet, VarRelationSet | replace_participant | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: P |
| All three birelation sets | replace_participant_1 | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: L1 |
| All three birelation sets | replace_participant_2 | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: L2 |

conveniences share the whole-factor mutation and incidence-update machinery;
they do not introduce separate index-maintenance paths.

Graph-IR and editor methods that delegate to these primitives still need
their own surface settled. This list introduces no new traits, wrapper types,
or Python bindings.

### Relation restoration

Relation storage owns two separate operation families across all five shapes:

| Name | Inputs and effect |
| --- | --- |
| restore | Relation compaction and saved removed rows. Recover original row positions, preserve surviving participants and attributes unchanged, and reinstate saved rows. No graph compaction is required. |
| restore_participants | GraphCompaction. Apply inverse reference translation to stored participants, preserving relation ids, participant positions, and attributes. |

Both operations maintain incidence indices. Compaction::uncompact and
RelationParticipant::uncompact already supply the inverse mappings; no new
participant trait operation is required. The existing editor restore_* methods
establish the restoration naming precedent. Its private restore_*_participants
helpers actually perform uncompaction, so their current names conflate the two
operations. The nomenclature guide distinguishes restoration of saved entities
from inverse mapping of survivors.

Compaction::uncompact and GraphCompaction::uncompact_node/uncompact_edge only
translate an id and return its original value; they do not mutate storage.
RelationParticipant::uncompact likewise returns a translated participant value.
Keep uncompaction non-mutating. Do not name a storage mutation
uncompact_participants. Applying translated references to stored participants and
updating incidence indices is the separate storage mutation restore_participants.

For topology rollback, first restore the surviving relations' participant references,
then restore removed relations. Saved removed rows already contain original
participant ids and must not be expanded again. Relation-only rollback needs
restore alone. Undoing participant replacement, insertion, or removal instead
uses the existing replace_participants methods with the saved factor contents;
any affected attributes are restored at the graph-IR level.

Exact rollback is bound to the removal history; restoration does not validate that
history again. A surviving payload changed since removal is preserved by restore;
recovering the original set requires original saved entries and unchanged survivors.

Existing removal APIs need not start returning journals: the transaction layer
can continue capturing rows before removal. Saved rows already use the set's
ordinary participant/payload shapes; a new storage journal type is not required
by this capability. Constraint restoration, session correspondence, cross-table
ordering, and transaction provenance remain graph-IR responsibilities.

### Graph restoration

Graph also owns restoration, through one bulk restore operation accepting a
GraphCompaction and saved removed edges with their original EdgeIds and endpoint
NodeIds. Nodes have no graph-owned payload, so the compaction supplies their
original positions and count. The operation restores the node id space, expands
surviving edge endpoints, restores original edge positions and saved edges, and
rebuilds adjacency storage. Graph continues to admit self-loops and parallel edges.

An identity compaction for the unaffected space permits node-only or edge-only
restoration. Separate restore_nodes and restore_edges conveniences are not required
for the initial capability. Graph owns both nodes and their internal edge references,
so restoring nodes includes translating surviving endpoints. Relation sets reference
an externally owned graph and keep participant-reference restoration separate
from row restoration.

Atom/bond attributes remain graph-IR-owned. The integration completed in 213
coordinates their restoration with Graph::restore, relation participant-reference
and row restoration, constraints, and correspondence.

### Approved interfaces and undo contract

All five relation-set types expose restore with &mut self, a borrowed
Compaction<RelationId>, and owned saved entries. For example, VarRelationSet:

```rust
pub fn restore(
    &mut self,
    compaction: &Compaction<RelationId>,
    removed: Vec<(RelationId, Vec<P>, D)>,
);
```

The original id precedes the existing constructor's entry fields:

| Storage shape | Saved entry |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed | `(RelationId, [P; N], D)` |
| Variable | `(RelationId, Vec<P>, D)` |
| Fixed/fixed | `(RelationId, [L1; N1], [L2; N2], D)` |
| Fixed/variable | `(RelationId, [L1; N1], Vec<L2>, D)` |
| Variable/variable | `(RelationId, Vec<L1>, Vec<L2>, D)` |

Entries may arrive in any order. Matching removal data covers compaction.removed()
exactly once and accompanies storage with compaction.result_count() surviving rows.
These relationships follow from removal and capture; restoration does not validate
them again. Explicit ids are retained rather than requiring entries to follow the
ordering of compaction.removed(). No new entry types are introduced solely for
these arguments.

Saved entries are consumed so payloads move into storage. Neither saved nor
surviving payloads require Clone. Participant order and payloads are preserved;
incidence is rebuilt for the restored relation ids. Relation restore retains the
ordinary storage admission contract for participant references: it does not
validate membership in an external graph.

All five relation-set shapes also expose:

```rust
pub fn restore_participants(
    &mut self,
    compaction: &GraphCompaction,
);
```

This applies to the whole set and both factors of birelations. Each referenced
node/edge from the matching removal belongs to the corresponding compaction result
domain. The method uses RelationParticipant::uncompact and preserves relation ids,
factor lengths, participant positions, and payloads while updating incidence. It
neither restores deleted participants nor reorders frames or transports payloads.

Graph exposes:

```rust
pub fn restore(
    &mut self,
    compaction: &GraphCompaction,
    removed: &[(EdgeId, [NodeId; 2])],
);
```

Matching removal data supplies the compaction and the original saved edges, in any
order. Saved endpoints use the original node id space. A borrowed slice suffices
because ids and endpoints are Copy; no node entries are needed.

All restoration methods return (). Their two requirements are:

1. Matching removal data reconstructs the original storage, including original ids.
2. Manipulated or mismatched inputs do not cause a panic. No correct restoration,
   diagnostic, or unchanged-receiver behavior is promised for those inputs.

Removal and capture establish the relationship between the receiver, compaction,
and saved entries. Separate arguments do not turn that undo operation into a
consumer of semantically independent inputs. This follows the operation-issued
provenance distinction in the [data-type guide](../docs/development/data-types.md#provenance-and-contextual-validity).
There is no RestoreError, checked counterpart, or tracked restoration variant.
The compaction already determines the survivor mapping.

### Guards against panics

Guard operations that could panic when undo data has been manipulated. Do not
re-establish the full removal history through a preliminary validation pass.

- Use checked inverse translation for graph survivors and protect saved-entry
  destination indexing. Check saved endpoint bounds before adjacency construction.
- Do not detect duplicates or require membership in the removed-id set. Exact
  current-count equality is not required merely to establish undo provenance.
- Handle missing rows without unwrapping absent entries or inventing generic
  payloads. No separate completeness scan or error-order contract is required.
- Before calling RelationParticipant::uncompact, inspect that participant's refs
  for coverage of the compaction result domain. Guard during traversal; do not
  add a separate whole-set validation pass or new participant trait method.
- Protect size arithmetic and allocation capacities from overflow. Keep internal
  storage coherent when stopping or skipping unusable input; the malformed-input
  contract does not specify which resulting storage value is chosen.

This replaces the earlier eleven-variant RestoreError and unchanged-on-error
proposal. Checks exist to prevent panics, not to diagnose compromised provenance.
The completed S6a work and initial S7a measurements are retained below as historical
records; the corrected implementation and measurements are recorded with S7a.

### Boundary cases and restoration laws

- Identity compaction with matching counts and empty saved entries makes restore
  a no-op. restore_participants with a matching identity also leaves storage unchanged.
- After complete removal, empty storage can restore the entire original graph or
  relation set. Graph restores isolated nodes from the compaction alone.
- Participant restoration on an empty relation set succeeds without changes;
  it does not recreate removed relations.

Matching removal and restoration must recover the original storage. Participant
compaction followed by restore_participants must recover surviving participant
values, with relation ids interpreted in the corresponding compacted row space.

The required graph-core additions are Graph::restore, restore and
restore_participants on all five relation-set types. Existing
uncompact interfaces and semantics remain unchanged. No new participant trait
methods, storage wrappers, or storage-owned transaction journals are needed. Edge-endpoint
replacement is a separate potential graph-core addition for bond rewiring,
not a restoration prerequisite.

## Entity-field invariant validation

Add one standalone, model-independent validator for the concrete domains of
inherent entity fields. It is not inserted into the public `Validator`
aggregate. Its settled contract is implemented by S13a below.

The check covers only literal inherent values:

- atom charge fits `i8`; implicit-hydrogen and lone-pair counts fit `u8`;
- localized-bond order is one of 1, 2, 3, or 4, and its charge fits `i8`;
- dative-bond order fits `u8`;
- aromatic-system and multicenter-bond charges fit `i8`.

`SpinInvariantsValidator` already owns unpaired-electron count, multiplicity,
and their physical compatibility for atoms, localized bonds, aromatic systems,
and multicenter bonds. Element, isotope, and noncovalent-kind fields already use
their existing typed representations. Stereo attribute compatibility and
electron-vector lengths remain open attribute properties, checked by operations
that require them; this focused numeric-field validator does not add those
checks. The current specification
does not define an additional entity-local charge/electron conservation law for
aromatic or multicenter entities, so this work does not invent one.

A relevant non-literal field makes this focused validation underdetermined; an
out-of-domain literal is a typed contradiction naming the entity, field, value,
and required domain. The validator neither mutates nor resolves the molecule
and does not traverse inline, relational, or molecule-level constraints.

The narrow `EntityFieldInvariantsValidator` name avoids reviving the old
`EntityStructureValidator`: the latter mixed checks that now belong to
`Molecule` integrity with semantic validation.

## Stereo-ligand invariant validation

Add a standalone `StereoLigandInvariantsValidator` to check the
model-independent requirement that a stereo entity lists every ligand of its
site. For each stereo-atom frame:

- its actual-atom ligands are exactly the site's localized-bond neighbors;
- its number of virtual implicit-hydrogen ligands equals the site's literal
  `implicit_hydrogens` count;
- its number of virtual lone-pair ligands equals the site's literal
  `lone_pairs` count.

Apply the same rules independently to each endpoint block of a stereo bond,
excluding the opposite site endpoint from that block's actual-atom ligands.
An explicit hydrogen is an ordinary actual-atom ligand: it is present by atom
id in the frame, is not included in the implicit-hydrogen count, and is not a
virtual ligand.

A non-literal inherent count makes the corresponding equality
underdetermined. A literal mismatch or an incomplete or extraneous
actual-atom ligand set produces a typed
`StereoLigandInvariantsContradiction` naming the stereo entity, bearer atom,
and mismatch. Representation integrity already limits each virtual-ligand kind
to zero or one occurrence within a stereo-atom frame or stereo-bond endpoint.
Consequently, an inherent implicit-hydrogen or lone-pair count greater than one
contradicts an existing stereo entity because that entity cannot contain the
complete ligand census. This validator imposes no corresponding restriction on
an atom that is not the bearer of a stereo entity.

The check is tier 2, not `Molecule` integrity. An incomplete or inconsistent
frame remains structurally interpretable and does not make ordinary graph-IR
operations unsafe; it is a model-independent semantic contradiction.
The existing integrity rule allowing at most one equal virtual ligand within a
stereo-atom frame or stereo-bond endpoint remains unchanged. Producers of
stereo entities, including stereo perception, must construct the same complete
census. They collect every actual ligand and add a virtual ligand for each
implicit-hydrogen or lone-pair count of exactly one. A count greater than one
precludes producing the stereo entity; it is not truncated to one virtual
ligand.

`HydrogenUnfolder` enforces the implicit-hydrogen requirement directly as an
operation precondition and returns `UnfoldHydrogensError` on disagreement. It
does not require a prior validator call and does not inspect lone-pair support,
which its transformation does not change.

S13b implements this standalone validator; hydrogen transformations do not
depend on a prior validation call.

## Constraint relocation

Decision revised 2026-10-18: move lifting and inlining out of the inherent
Molecule API in graph IR and into separate Transformer implementations under
umol-graph's ops::transform. The engine names are ConstraintLifter and
ConstraintInliner. Transform supplies lift_constraints / lift_constraints_into
and inline_constraints / inline_constraints_into; Python follows the same
receiver-method design as the other transformations. Neither operation runs
automatically during resolution, construction, or serialization.

Lifting must relocate every meaningful inline assertion or return an error.
Leaving an unliftable assertion inline is not successful lifting. Existing
molecule-level entries remain, and successful lifting leaves every inline store
empty. The established exception is kindless stereo: its inline constraints
are vacuous and are discarded without interpreting or normalizing their
payloads. A known kind with an undetermined coset retains meaningful constraints
and follows the ordinary lifting path.

Entity attributes permit values that molecule-level stereo wrappers cannot
represent: a known kind can disagree with the ligand-frame arity, or an inline
constraint can contain an incompatible permutation or ligand position. Lifting
reports the existing representation-integrity failure when such a payload
cannot be relocated. It does not repair the frame, reinterpret the payload,
or require a determined coset. An empty inline store creates no wrapper and
requires no additional stereo check. Attribute mutation remains infallible.

Inlining must move every bare top-level entity assertion into its respective
store or return an error. Partial inlining is not a successful result: an
assertion that cannot be moved must not be left at molecule level while the
others are inlined. Entries with the same key combine by meet, including
entries moved earlier in the same operation; bottom rejects the whole operation.
Logical subtrees, relational constraints, and molecule predicates remain at
molecule level in their stored order; they are outside the relocation's scope.
Inlining is not a traversal that extracts leaves from logical expressions.

A molecule-level stereo assertion carries a kind; its inline counterpart gets
that meaning from the entity's configuration. The current implementation drops
the carried kind. For a meaningful assertion, the replacement must require
agreement before relocation. A different or undetermined entity kind cannot
absorb that assertion without changing its meaning and must cause an error.
Do not change the entity's kind or silently discard the assertion to make
inlining succeed.

Both engines preserve topology, entity ids, inherent fields, configurations,
and ligand frames. Consuming failure drops the input; in-place failure leaves
the input unchanged. Preparation produces a checked candidate before replacing
the caller's borrowed molecule. Discharge is the separate operation for removing
proven redundancy.

Preparation decision, 2026-10-18: use the existing public graph-IR editor
publication boundary from umol-graph. Perform the complete relocation in a
MoleculeEditor and call finish once to obtain the checked candidate. Its full
integrity check can reject an unliftable payload before the candidate is
published to the caller. Do not call probe followed by finish and repeat the
check, or replay the relocation after successfully preparing the result.

The consuming path moves the input into the editor. The _into path prepares a
working copy and replaces the caller's molecule only after finish succeeds.
Cloning shares backing storage; modifying shared stores can copy their
contents. Both paths pay for a full integrity check, including unchanged
topology and overlays. These costs belong in the bounded comparison for S14.

No broader public tier-1 checking API or duplicate integrity implementation is
needed for this work. MoleculeEditor::finish returns MoleculeIntegrityError;
lifting translates the corresponding stereo-admission failures into
LiftConstraintsError. S14 replaces the former local kindless-panic correction
with this migration.

## Counts-resolver valence capacity

Recorded 2026-09-10 during the doc 226 stereo-input experiments. With
ValenceModel::smiles() (counts candidate generation and MostSaturated), both
`[C-](F)(Cl)(Br)(I)` and `[C@-](F)(Cl)(Br)(I)` were observed to resolve with zero
hydrogens, zero lone pairs, and one unpaired electron on carbon. Four bonds
plus that electron put nine electrons around carbon. Both inputs must be
rejected by valence resolution, independently of the stereo marker.

In `umol-graph/src/ops/valence/counts.rs`, literal hydrogen counts bypass the
saturation-target bonding budget; candidate generation balances the remaining
electron budget but admits a count above Element::valence_capacity. Saturation targets
are hydrogen-completion targets, not an allowed-valence whitelist. The fix
must enforce that bound without conflating those contracts.

The correction uses Element::valence_capacity, which already supplies
the per-element bound in umol-chem. CountsValence uses this data to bound lone
pairs but does not yet bound the total electron count. Use the actual
atom's element for this bound, not the shifted element used for table lookup.
With the existing electron accounting in invariant::electron_count, the total is:

```text
valence_electrons - charge + localized_valence + implicit_hydrogens
    + aromatic_covalence(aromatic_valence) + 2 * accepted_pairs
```

Reject a candidate when this total exceeds its element's valence_capacity,
whether its hydrogen count was supplied or inferred. For the four-coordinate
carbon anion, the total is 4 - (-1) + 4 = 9 against capacity 8. Use each
element's existing bound; no new element table is needed.

Apply the bound in CountsValence::candidate_states and in its separate
infer_implicit_hydrogens path used by SMILES export. classify_molecule_atom
already uses candidate_states. Empty candidate sets retain CountsError::NoMatch;
hydrogen inference retains None when no admissible count exists. Resolution's
existing treatment of already-complete atoms is unchanged. The check uses
already-read values and adds scalar arithmetic and a comparison per candidate,
without an allocation or another molecule traversal.

Regression coverage must include both marked and unmarked reproducers,
valid neutral four-coordinate carbon, three-coordinate carbanions, aromatic
and dative accounting, and a ClF3 control using chlorine's bound. Two existing
expectations directly conflict with the bound: neutral divalent fluorine and
hydrogen inference on oxygen with charge -4 must become rejections. Saturation
targets remain completion targets rather than a whitelist of allowed valences.
S15a implements and verifies this correction.

## Molecule transformation interface

Design agreed 2026-10-18; implementation has not started.

Add one public extension trait, Transform, in umol_graph::ops::transform,
implemented for Molecule. It supplies named receiver methods for the
transformation family. Keeping the methods together gives callers one trait
import for these operations.

| Operation | Consuming Rust method | In-place method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aromatization | aromatize | aromatize_into |
| Kekulization | kekulize | kekulize_into |
| Charge delocalization | delocalize_charge | delocalize_charge_into |
| Hydrogen folding | fold_hydrogens | fold_hydrogens_into |
| Hydrogen unfolding | unfold_hydrogens | unfold_hydrogens_into |
| Constraint lifting | lift_constraints | lift_constraints_into |
| Constraint inlining | inline_constraints | inline_constraints_into |
| Constraint discharge | discharge_constraints | discharge_constraints_into |

Each method delegates to the corresponding transformer engine, retaining its
operation inputs and error semantics. The Rust base method consumes self and
returns the transformed molecule; the _into method takes &mut self and
publishes in place. A consuming rejection drops the input; an in-place
rejection leaves the input unchanged.

Transformer remains the common interface implemented by operation objects,
including transform, transform_into, and transform_iter. Transform supplies
the Molecule-facing methods; Transformer supplies the engine-facing methods.
The receiver interface complements the existing engines.

Composition uses ordinary sequential calls. Each operation works against the
current molecule and applies and publishes independently. If a later in-place
operation fails, the earlier successful operations remain applied. A composite
transformer is not required for this interface.

Python initially exposes the same named methods directly on Molecule, without
a transformer class hierarchy. The base methods preserve the receiver and
return an independently mutable Molecule, following the established Python
Molecule ownership contract: clone the Rust receiver before invoking the
consuming operation. The initial clone shares storage; modified shared data
is copied as needed. The _into methods mutate the receiver and return None.
Operation failures raise Python exceptions. A caller can copy once and use
successive _into calls to avoid repeated copying in a sequence.

Transform has a Sized bound. Its consuming methods return Result<Self, E>,
and the corresponding _into methods return Result<(), E>. The implementation
for Molecule uses these arguments and engine errors:

| Method pair | Arguments after self / &mut self | E |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aromatize / aromatize_into | model: &AromaticityModel, config: AromaticityConfig | AromatizeError |
| kekulize / kekulize_into | node_order: Vec<AtomId>, config: KekulizeConfig | KekulizeError |

has the same arguments:

### Storage mutation implementation strategy

Initially, whole-factor replacement writes the selected row's participant
storage and rebuilds the derived Incidence once from the final stored rows.
Fixed factors replace their arrays; variable factors replace their contiguous
range and adjust subsequent offsets. Payload storage and relation numbering
remain untouched. Rebuilding incidence does not require reconstructing the
relation set through new, cloning payloads, or materializing editor entries.

For a birelation, the both-factor method is the mutation foundation. The
factor-specific methods preserve the other factor and delegate to it. Every
single-participant method constructs the changed factor and delegates to its
whole-factor method. Both-factor replacement rebuilds incidence only after
both final factors are installed. Keep the existing multiset-preserving
permutation implementation; this delegation requirement concerns the new
mutation family.

This deliberately starts with the existing incidence builder. Its cost depends
on the entire collection, and variable-factor resizing may move later
participants. Measure those costs explicitly; do not claim constant-time
replacement or representative molecular workloads from synthetic benchmarks.
Incremental incidence maintenance and specialized single-participant mutation
are follow-up optimizations, subject to evidence and a separate decision.

### S5 — Add and remove whole relations — approved 2026-09-17

S5a–S5e are complete as of 2026-09-18. Each of the five relation sets now provides
add, remove, and tracked_remove. This stage covers storage only; editor wiring
and its public mutation surface remain separate follow-up work.

Every add takes &mut self, followed by the arguments below, and returns RelationId:

| Set | Arguments after &mut self |
| --- | --- |
| FixedRelationSet | participants: [P; N], data: D |
| VarRelationSet | participants: &[P], data: D |
| FixedFixedBirelationSet | participants_1: [L1; N1], participants_2: [L2; N2], data: D |
| FixedVarBirelationSet | participants_1: [L1; N1], participants_2: &[L2], data: D |
| VarVarBirelationSet | participants_1: &[L1], participants_2: &[L2], data: D |

Addition appends one relation and returns the previous count as its id. Existing
ids, participants, and payloads remain unchanged. The supplied participant order
and multiplicity are preserved; coinciding relations are permitted. Fixed arity
is enforced by the argument type, and variable factors may be empty. References
are indexed through RelationParticipant::refs without checking membership in an
external graph, matching new and participant replacement. Incidence is correct
before return and spans both factors of a birelation. The payload is moved into
storage; no D: Clone bound is introduced. There is no tracked addition variant:
the append layout and returned id fully describe the id change.

All five sets have the same removal signatures:

```rust
pub fn remove(&mut self, ids: &[RelationId]);

pub fn tracked_remove(&mut self, ids: &[RelationId]) -> Compaction<RelationId>;
```

Removal interprets every supplied id in the pre-removal set. Input order and
repetitions do not matter. Survivors retain their relative order, participant
sequences, and payloads; their relation ids become dense, and incidence uses
those new ids. Participant node/edge ids do not change. Empty input is a no-op,
with an identity compaction over the original count. Any out-of-range relation
id panics; validate all supplied ids before mutation. Plain remove delegates to
tracked_remove and discards its compaction. Single-relation removal uses &[id].
No payload Clone bound is needed. The returned compaction records the source
count and survivor mapping, not removed payloads or an undo journal.

These methods remove whole relations. Existing remove_participant methods edit
a factor within one relation. Removing a relation does not remove its referenced
nodes or edges. Existing compact/tracked_compact handle graph compaction and the
resulting removal of dependent relations.

Initially, storage updates its columns and variable-factor offsets directly,
then rebuilds Incidence once per addition or nonempty removal batch. It must not
reconstruct the set through new or clone payloads. Measure the complete mutation,
including the rebuild; repeated additions must not be described as constant-time.
Keep the approved module organization and visibility unchanged.

#### S6c uncompaction lookup and remeasurement — 2026-09-18

Compaction::try_uncompact now finds the source-id offset with one binary
search and no allocation. For sorted, distinct removed ids r[i], r[i] - i
counts surviving source ids before that removal. These counts are nondecreasing,
including equal counts across a contiguous removed run. The removals that precede
result id p are exactly those with r[i] - i <= p. Adding their number to p
therefore recovers its source id. The existing result-domain check still runs
before the search. Representation, trait bounds, constructor behavior, inverse
semantics, and None/panic behavior are unchanged; all existing wrappers delegate
to the optimized lookup.

  losslessness or inverse-roundtrip claim.
