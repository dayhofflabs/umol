## Scope

This document tracks two additive operations over `Molecule`: conversion between
implicit and explicit hydrogen representations, and validation of the concrete
domains of inherent entity fields. It carries forward the still-relevant parts
of Phase 11a and Phase 12 from doc 086 using the current model.

It also tracks the deferred counts-resolver shell-capacity correction below.

The operation boundaries are:

- resolution fills undetermined state using a chemistry model;
- transformation rewrites one resolved representation into another;
- validation checks integrity, model-independent invariants, and
  model-dependent conformance without mutation.

The boundaries and validator composition established by docs 096 and 118 remain
in force. Correspondence-returning hydrogen variants belong to the separate
mutation-witness work. New or revised molecule-level constraint validation,
including unpaired-electron coupling constraints, is deferred to doc 195.

`ConnectivityModel` is already part of `ChemistryModel`, and
`ConnectivityConformanceValidator` already runs first in conformance
validation. Constraint invariants, aromaticity conformance, spin invariants,
overlay uniqueness, and aromatic/multicenter electron-vector shape are also
implemented. This document does not retain parallel work items for them.

## Implicit and explicit hydrogen transformations

Add two high-level transformer types under `umol-graph/src/ops/transform`, each
implementing `Transformer`. They are the ordinary public operation surface and
need no configuration:

- `HydrogenFolder` folds eligible explicit hydrogen atoms and their bonds into
  implicit-hydrogen counts;
- `HydrogenUnfolder` unfolds literal implicit-hydrogen counts into explicit
  hydrogen atoms and bonds.

The complete rewrite of topology, entity ids, stereo ligand frames, and stored
references belongs to graph IR. Expose the narrowly required low-level
`fold_explicit_hydrogens` and `unfold_implicit_hydrogens` functions from
`umol-graph-ir`; the `umol-graph` transformers delegate to them and own the
high-level operation contract. Their operation errors are
`FoldHydrogensError` and `UnfoldHydrogensError`. Do not duplicate the rewrite
in the chemistry layer or replace the transformers with inherent `Molecule`
methods.

### Public API shape

### API contract

- The operation objects are stateless engines, not carriers of validated state.
  Their input and output are ordinary closed `Molecule` values.
- The graph-IR functions return fresh molecules. `transform` delegates to the
  corresponding function, while `transform_into` computes a complete candidate
  and replaces its argument only on success. An error therefore leaves every
  caller-visible input unchanged.
- The first folding or unfolding operation that depends on a nonnegative
  literal implicit-hydrogen count or a consistent stereo ligand census checks
  that precondition itself. A prior validator call is not required.
- The validators inspect model-independent semantic predicates. They neither
  construct a stronger molecule type nor transform, repair, or resolve their
  input. A relevant non-literal field produces `Solution::Underdetermined`; a
  literal violation produces `Solution::Contradictory`.
- `generate_all` follows the existing deterministic-transformer contract: it
  yields the one transformed molecule on success and no item on error. Callers
  that require the typed diagnostic use `transform` or `transform_into`.
- Both transformations preserve `Molecule` representation integrity and are
  idempotent. No inverse, lossless, or general roundtrip law is exposed.
- This work adds no conversions, prepared or validated wrappers,
  correspondence-returning variants, or Python bindings.

### Expansion to explicit hydrogens

- A literal implicit-hydrogen count must be nonnegative; return
  `UnfoldHydrogensError` atomically for a negative count. For `n > 0`, add `n`
  hydrogen atoms and `n` localized single bonds; zero is unchanged.
- Each added hydrogen has the concrete form
  `H#i=#c0#h0#n0#u0#s`.
- Set the source atom's implicit-hydrogen field to literal zero.
- Leave undetermined or non-literal counts unchanged.
- Leave already explicit hydrogens unchanged; mixed explicit and implicit
  hydrogen representations therefore become fully explicit wherever the
  implicit count is literal.
- A stereo-atom frame and each stereo-bond endpoint contain a complete ligand
  census, not a selected subset. For every affected frame or endpoint, its
  number of virtual implicit-hydrogen ligands must equal the bearer's literal
  implicit-hydrogen count. A stereo entity therefore cannot exist on a bearer
  with more than one implicit hydrogen: the complete frame would require
  repeated implicit-hydrogen ligands, which representation integrity
  prohibits.
- Create explicit hydrogens once per bearer atom. Replace every virtual
  implicit-hydrogen occurrence anchored to that bearer with the corresponding
  newly explicit hydrogen. An atom with a literal count greater than one can
  still be unfolded when it is not the bearer of a stereo entity.
- Return `UnfoldHydrogensError` when an affected stereo frame or endpoint
  disagrees with a literal implicit-hydrogen count, or when a virtual ligand is
  backed by a non-literal count. An atom with a non-literal count and no such
  virtual ligand remains unchanged.
- Reapplying the transformer is a no-op.

### Collapse to implicit hydrogens

Collapse a hydrogen only when it is an unambiguous ordinary explicit hydrogen:

- exactly one incident localized bond;
- the bond has the concrete form `1#c0#u0#s` and no bond constraints;
- non-hydrogen partner;
- no isotope label, charge, spin, or other non-default atom state;
- no atom constraints;
- neither the hydrogen nor its bond is referenced by a molecule constraint;
- no participation in dative, aromatic, multicenter, or noncovalent relations.

The partner's implicit-hydrogen count must be a nonnegative literal. An
undetermined count does not authorize deleting an explicit hydrogen; leave
that hydrogen unchanged. A negative literal produces `FoldHydrogensError`
atomically. Otherwise remove the hydrogen and bond and increment the partner's
implicit-hydrogen count. No separate checked-arithmetic or overflow error is
part of this operation's contract.

An explicit hydrogen used as a stereo-atom or stereo-bond ligand is replaced
in place by an implicit-hydrogen ligand on its non-hydrogen partner. If the
complete affected stereo frames cannot represent the result without repeating
an implicit-hydrogen ligand, return `FoldHydrogensError` atomically. This
includes a pre-existing implicit hydrogen and multiple explicit hydrogen
ligands that would be folded onto the same stereo bearer. The transformer does
not remove the stereo entity to make the fold possible.

Preserve D/T, hydrides/protons, radicals, H2, other relation participants, and
any hydrogen whose information could not be represented after collapse. Mixed
explicit and literal implicit hydrogens are combined in the implicit count
when no stereo entity prevents it. Reapplying the transformer is a no-op.

No general losslessness or inverse roundtrip law is claimed. Folding removes
atom and bond identities, and mixed or non-foldable explicit representations
preclude a universal inverse law. Each operation returns only the transformed
`Molecule`; the correspondence or compaction that witnesses the change is
separate mutation-witness work.

Verification must cover per-direction behavior, idempotence, field preservation,
relation preservation, representative reconstructible cases, and every error
boundary. Do not state a general roundtrip property.

## Stereo ligand mutation

Both hydrogen transformations require a position-preserving substitution in a
stereo frame: unfolding replaces a virtual implicit-hydrogen ligand with the
new explicit hydrogen atom, while folding makes the inverse representation
change. The ligand occupying the position changes, but the position itself and
the configuration read against the frame do not.

This is distinct from both existing frame operations. `FrameTransport` applies
a supplied frame action, while `Reframe` selects the canonical representative
frame. Both preserve the ligand multiset, transport the frame-relative form,
and reorder the stored ligands through `FixedVarBirelationSet::permute_2_with`.
Ligand substitution preserves the frame position but changes the participant
multiset. It may therefore change the derived incidence index: replacing a
virtual ligand anchored to one atom with an actual ligand naming a newly added
hydrogen changes which atom ids the stereo entity references.

The current immutable graph-IR readers are
`StereoAtoms::ligands(id) -> &[StereoLigand]` and
`StereoBonds::ligands(id) -> &[StereoLigand]`. Their storage is backed by the
second factor of `FixedVarBirelationSet`, whose participants are immutable
except for multiset-preserving permutation. `MoleculeEditor` instead
materializes a touched stereo family into owned entries and rebuilds indexed
relation storage when the molecule is published.

### Storage-owned mutation decision — 2026-09-15

Participant mutation belongs to relation storage. The editor must delegate to
that capability rather than use materialized entries as a substitute for it.
The prerequisite covers all five relation-set shapes: FixedRelationSet,
VarRelationSet, FixedFixedBirelationSet, FixedVarBirelationSet, and
VarVarBirelationSet. The earlier proposal to expose mutable ligand references
from the editor is withdrawn; a bare mutable reference cannot give indexed
storage control over completion of the replacement.

Design whole-factor replacement, single-participant replacement, insertion,
and removal together. Whole-factor replacement is the foundation: fixed
factors retain their arity, variable factors accept a new sequence, and
birelations can replace either factor or both together. Implement the complete
method family initially through whole-factor replacement; specialize later
only if justified.

Replacement preserves the relation id, payload, and supplied participant order.
It updates incidence over the complete relation, including both factors of a
birelation. Incidence is a union of references: replacing one participant must
retain an old node or edge incidence if another participant still references
it. This matters when a virtual stereo ligand and its site share an atom
reference. Graph core neither interprets the payload nor enforces molecular
semantics. Graph IR owns the coordinated topology, ligand, and payload changes
and the molecule publication boundary.

A single helper that scans and rewrites both stereo-atom and stereo-bond
frames is not the intended graph-IR abstraction. The entity families remain
explicit. Hydrogen substitution preserves positions and their configuration;
arbitrary participant replacement makes no such semantic promise. Folding must
substitute hydrogen ligands before topology removal, whose existing cascade
would otherwise remove stereo entities still referencing those hydrogens.

### Approved graph-core additions — 2026-09-15

The following methods are approved public additions in graph core. Every
method takes &mut self followed by id: RelationId; the tables list the
remaining arguments. All return (). An invalid relation id panics, consistently
with existing indexed access. An invalid factor-local position also panics:
replacement/removal require position < length; insertion permits position <=
length.

Participant values follow the same admission contract as new. NodeId and
EdgeId references inside participants are stored and indexed through
RelationParticipant::refs without checking membership in an external graph.
The relation set has no supplied graph or declared node/edge domain against
which to validate them. Repeated participant values remain representable;
incidence deduplicates references, not the stored participant sequence. Fixed
factor lengths are enforced by array types; variable factors permit any length,
including zero. There is no new Result, error type, or checked variant.

#### Single-participant operations

All of these operations are included in the initial implementation. They
construct the replacement factor and delegate to whole-factor replacement.

| Available on | Methods | Arguments after id |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FixedRelationSet, VarRelationSet | replace_participant | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: P |
| All three birelation sets | replace_participant_1 | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: L1 |
| All three birelation sets | replace_participant_2 | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: L2 |
| VarRelationSet | insert_participant | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: P |
| VarRelationSet | remove_participant | position: ParticipantPosition |
| FixedVarBirelationSet, VarVarBirelationSet | insert_participant_2 | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: L2 |
| FixedVarBirelationSet, VarVarBirelationSet | remove_participant_2 | position: ParticipantPosition |
| VarVarBirelationSet | insert_participant_1 | position: ParticipantPosition, participant: L1 |
| VarVarBirelationSet | remove_participant_1 | position: ParticipantPosition |

Positions are local to the selected factor. Replacement and removal address
an existing position. Insertion occurs before the selected position and
accepts the factor length to append. Insertion and removal preserve the
relative order of surviving participants and are available only for variable
factors. Single-participant replacement preserves factor length. These
conveniences share the whole-factor mutation and incidence-update machinery;
they do not introduce separate index-maintenance paths.

Graph-IR and editor methods that delegate to these primitives still need
their own surface settled. This list introduces no new traits, wrapper types,
or Python bindings.

#### Implementation strategy

Initially, whole-factor replacement writes the selected row's participant
storage and rebuilds the derived Incidence once from the final stored rows.
Fixed factors replace their arrays; variable factors replace their contiguous
range and adjust subsequent offsets. Payload storage and relation numbering
remain untouched. Rebuilding incidence does not require reconstructing the
relation set through new, cloning payloads, or materializing editor entries.

For a birelation, the both-factor method is the mutation foundation. The
factor-specific methods preserve the other factor and delegate to it. Every
single-participant method constructs the changed factor and delegates to its
whole-factor method. Both-factor replacement rebuilds incidence only after
both final factors are installed. Keep the existing multiset-preserving
permutation implementation; this delegation requirement concerns the new
mutation family.

This deliberately starts with the existing incidence builder. Its cost depends
on the entire collection, and variable-factor resizing may move later
participants. Measure those costs explicitly; do not claim constant-time
replacement or representative molecular workloads from synthetic benchmarks.
Incremental incidence maintenance and specialized single-participant mutation
are follow-up optimizations, subject to evidence and a separate decision.

## Entity-field invariant validation

Add one standalone, model-independent validator for the concrete domains of
inherent entity fields. The validator is additive in 0.7.1: it is not inserted
into the public `Validator` aggregate, whose public fields and contradiction
enum make that integration breaking.

The check covers only literal inherent values:

- atom charge fits `i8`; implicit-hydrogen and lone-pair counts fit `u8`;
- localized-bond order is one of 1, 2, 3, or 4, and its charge fits `i8`;
- dative-bond order fits `u8`;
- aromatic-system and multicenter-bond charges fit `i8`.

`SpinInvariantsValidator` already owns unpaired-electron count, multiplicity,
and their physical compatibility for atoms, localized bonds, aromatic systems,
and multicenter bonds. Element, isotope, noncovalent-kind, and stereo domains
are already represented by bounded types or enforced by `Molecule` integrity.
Electron-vector length is also an integrity property. The current specification
does not define an additional entity-local charge/electron conservation law for
aromatic or multicenter entities, so this work does not invent one.

A relevant non-literal field makes this focused validation underdetermined; an
out-of-domain literal is a typed contradiction naming the entity, field, value,
and required domain. The validator neither mutates nor resolves the molecule
and does not traverse inline, relational, or molecule-level constraints.

The narrow `EntityFieldInvariantsValidator` name avoids reviving the old
`EntityStructureValidator`: the latter mixed checks that now belong to
`Molecule` integrity with semantic validation.

## Stereo-ligand invariant validation

Add a standalone `StereoLigandInvariantsValidator` to check the
model-independent requirement that a stereo entity lists every ligand of its
site. For each stereo-atom frame:

- its actual-atom ligands are exactly the site's localized-bond neighbors;
- its number of virtual implicit-hydrogen ligands equals the site's literal
  `implicit_hydrogens` count;
- its number of virtual lone-pair ligands equals the site's literal
  `lone_pairs` count.

Apply the same rules independently to each endpoint block of a stereo bond,
excluding the opposite site endpoint from that block's actual-atom ligands.
An explicit hydrogen is an ordinary actual-atom ligand: it is present by atom
id in the frame, is not included in the implicit-hydrogen count, and is not a
virtual ligand.

A non-literal inherent count makes the corresponding equality
underdetermined. A literal mismatch or an incomplete or extraneous
actual-atom ligand set produces a typed
`StereoLigandInvariantsContradiction` naming the stereo entity, bearer atom,
and mismatch. Representation integrity already limits each virtual-ligand kind
to zero or one occurrence within a stereo-atom frame or stereo-bond endpoint.
Consequently, an inherent implicit-hydrogen or lone-pair count greater than one
contradicts an existing stereo entity because that entity cannot contain the
complete ligand census. This validator imposes no corresponding restriction on
an atom that is not the bearer of a stereo entity.

The check is tier 2, not `Molecule` integrity. An incomplete or inconsistent
frame remains structurally interpretable and does not make ordinary graph-IR
operations unsafe; it is a model-independent semantic contradiction.
The existing integrity rule allowing at most one equal virtual ligand within a
stereo-atom frame or stereo-bond endpoint remains unchanged. Producers of
stereo entities, including stereo perception, must construct the same complete
census. They collect every actual ligand and add a virtual ligand for each
implicit-hydrogen or lone-pair count of exactly one. A count greater than one
precludes producing the stereo entity; it is not truncated to one virtual
ligand.

`HydrogenUnfolder` enforces the implicit-hydrogen requirement directly as an
operation precondition and returns `UnfoldHydrogensError` on disagreement. It
does not require a prior validator call and does not inspect lone-pair support,
which its transformation does not change.

## Deferred counts-resolver shell-capacity correction

Recorded 2026-09-10 during the doc 226 stereo-input experiments. With
ValenceModel::smiles() (counts candidate generation and MostSaturated), both
`[C-](F)(Cl)(Br)(I)` and `[C@-](F)(Cl)(Br)(I)` currently resolve with zero
hydrogens, zero lone pairs, and one unpaired electron on carbon. Four bonds
plus that electron put nine electrons around carbon. Both inputs must be
rejected by valence resolution, independently of the stereo marker.

In `umol-graph/src/ops/valence/counts.rs`, literal hydrogen counts bypass the
saturation-target bonding budget; candidate generation balances the remaining
electron budget but admits this shell-capacity violation. Saturation targets
are hydrogen-completion targets, not an allowed-valence whitelist. The fix
must enforce the applicable shell capacity without conflating those contracts.
The design and implementation remain deferred; do not generalize carbon's
eight-electron bound to every element.

The scratch scan and exact outcomes are in
`scratch/stereo-valence-scan/report.md` and `results.jsonl`. Future regression
coverage must include both marked and unmarked reproducers and valid neutral
four-coordinate carbon and three-coordinate carbanion controls. Differences
in the layer rejecting other stereo inputs do not require changes for this
work item. This correction is separate from, and does not block, the SMILES
roundtrip design.
